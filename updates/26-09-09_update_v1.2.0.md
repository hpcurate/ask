## What was done

A **quick mode** switch in settings. With it on, a *quick add* button appears on
the write screen and asks for one thing at a time, in order: the request, its
kind, the project, the area, the priority, then an optional note.

**Answering is advancing.** A chips step takes one key — a digit picks the
option with that number, or the cursor keys move the highlight and the act key
takes it — and there is no confirm button to reach for afterwards. A text step
takes Enter, and its field is focused with the caret at the end the instant the
step lands, so it is ready to type into. That is where "extremely smooth" is
actually spent: six questions, six keys, no travel between them.

**Escape goes back one step, not out.** Losing four answers to one mistyped key
is how a fast path stops getting used.

### It is not a second form

Every step writes into the same `draft` the long form uses, and the last one
files through the same `readForm()` and `Store.add()`. There is one definition
of what a request is, so the two cannot drift — and the queue, the editing and
the Todoist contract all behave identically whichever way it was written.

### The one place it hands back

Picking **other** as the project needs a name typed, and there is nowhere in the
flow to type one. Rather than inventing a seventh step nobody asked for, it
closes, carries the title across to the long form and puts the cursor in the
project field.

### Look

A full-height sheet rather than a small dialog: one question at a time earns the
whole screen, and the point is that nothing else is competing for attention. The
step slides up as it lands and that is the only motion — anything more and six
questions in six seconds becomes six animations you wait through. A row of
progress dots across the top, and the keys are named under each question.
